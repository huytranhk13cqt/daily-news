## 1. Cloudflare Research Argues ML-DSA Is Still the Only Deployable Post-Quantum Signature Until 2029

| Field | Value |

### 🔭 Overview (Tổng)
Bas Westerbaan and Christopher Patton published on 09/07/2026 a Cloudflare Research post arguing that despite the nine new post-quantum signature candidates NIST is currently evaluating, ML-DSA remains the only algorithm that data-plane engineers can actually plan a WebPKI rollout around this decade. The piece is a follow-on to Cloudflare's earlier post-quantum key-exchange coverage and lands as certificate authorities, browsers and cloud load balancers begin scoping their crypto agility budgets. For any team that terminates TLS in front of a data platform — dbt Cloud, Snowflake, Databricks, Kafka mTLS or an internal service mesh — this is the piece of research that maps how much larger those handshakes are about to get and when the alternatives finally arrive.

### 🔬 Details (Phân)
The concrete numbers matter for planning. ML-DSA-44 uses a 1,312-byte public key and 2,420-byte signatures, both of which are already large enough to fragment across TCP segments at typical MTUs and to visibly slow the TLS 1.3 handshake compared with today's ECDSA. The competing candidates each trade one axis for another. FN-DSA-512 cuts signatures to 666 bytes with an 897-byte public key but signs about 3x slower than ML-DSA and pushes side-channel countermeasures into every implementation. SQIsign is compact — 65-byte public keys and 148-byte signatures — but signs 300x slower than ML-DSA, which is a non-starter for a QUIC handshake. UOV variants would compress signatures to 96 bytes but require 66 kB public keys, which is a certificate size regression that no CDN would ship.

The timeline reasoning is where the argument bites. FN-DSA is projected to be product-ready in 2033, multivariate schemes in 2034, SQIsign in 2035 or later, and proof-of-knowledge schemes in 2030 at the earliest. ML-DSA achieved final NIST standardization in August 2024 and WebPKI deployment is expected in early 2027. The authors quote Eric Rescorla — "You go to war with the algorithms you have, not the ones you wish you had" — and use it to frame the practical position: waiting for smaller, faster candidates means shipping nothing while stored-and-decrypted traffic accumulates a longer harvest-now-decrypt-later exposure window. The unstated corollary for data engineers is that certificate and JWT-signing formats will need to accommodate the ML-DSA blob for at least a decade before any migration to a smaller scheme becomes credible.

### 🎯 Takeaway (Hợp)
Start budgeting header, cert-chain and QUIC INITIAL frame sizes for ML-DSA-44 now rather than betting on FN-DSA to arrive before 2029; the piece is Cloudflare telling the industry the "small PQ signature" story is not going to save you. Trước khi khoá vào bất kỳ SDK ký JWT nào, kiểm tra xem library có kế hoạch cho ML-DSA và cho signature 2.4 KB không.

### 💡 Example

```sql
-- Rough shape of the ClickHouse landing table Trigger.dev's write-side sketch implies:
-- runs come in as one row per execution with dynamic attributes as JSON.
CREATE TABLE runs_events
(
    project_id   LowCardinality(String),
    run_id       String,
    ts           DateTime64(3, 'UTC'),
    event_type   LowCardinality(String),
    duration_ms  UInt32,
    status       LowCardinality(String),
    attrs        JSON
)
ENGINE = MergeTree
PARTITION BY toStartOfDay(ts)
ORDER BY (project_id, event_type, ts)
TTL ts + INTERVAL 30 DAY DELETE;

-- Aggregated usage metric backed by an incremental materialised view.
CREATE MATERIALIZED VIEW runs_usage_daily
ENGINE = SummingMergeTree
ORDER BY (project_id, toDate(ts))
AS SELECT project_id, toDate(ts) AS day, count() AS runs,
          sum(duration_ms) AS total_ms
FROM runs_events GROUP BY project_id, day;
```

### 🔬 Details (Phân)

Compared with commercial RTLS bundles from Ubisense or Sewio, which start in the thousands of dollars, the DIY story is now credible for home and small-office use cases. The trade-off worth flagging is the calibration burden: UWB anchors need known 3D positions and time-of-flight synchronisation, and ESPHome's tooling for that is still evolving.

### 🎯 Takeaway (Hợp)
