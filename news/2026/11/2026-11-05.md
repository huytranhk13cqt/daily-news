### 🔭 Overview (Tổng)

Google assesses that an additional at-least 10 million devices will be protected by its ongoing actions. The combined operation names two components — `x.cpe` as the primary backdoor, `com.abc.nexus` as a loader and execution engine — and documents a reinstall-on-reboot path from the `/oem/` partition when a user removes the payload. Synthient tested the MECOOL KM9Pro and compiled a non-exhaustive list of infected devices on GitHub.

### 🔬 Details (Phân)
The structural fact of this incident is that `x.cpe` is preloaded in the firmware partition, not dropped at runtime. If a user deletes it, Google's report says it "automatically reinstalls from the /oem/ partition upon reboot," which makes consumer-side removal of the payload transitory without reflashing. Google's framing is that Videostrong used obfuscation so the malicious code only loaded after first boot, which evaded pre-shipment security testing — specifically relevant because these are Google-certified devices, meaning the Play Protect pipeline signed them after a test regime that the obfuscation defeated. The article names only the MECOOL KM9Pro as explicitly tested, with a caveat that for Videostrong or MECOOL devices "it's probably safe to assume your box is infected." Operators of fleets that include either brand should treat this as a firmware-level incident, not an app-level one.

Command-and-control runs over HTTP and MQTT, which is unusual enough to flag: MQTT in a residential endpoint is close to a fingerprint. Google states that most of the malware was hosted on GitHub, with the remainder on operator-controlled servers and file-hosting sites, and the company has pursued legal action against the command-and-control domains and cut ties with Videostrong. The affiliate modules monetise through residential-proxy and ad-fraud participation, which for enterprise networks matters because a compromised device becomes an egress point for third-party traffic and a fraud generator — both are detectable at the network layer if you scan for the MQTT fingerprint and for the GitHub-hosted payload URLs Google's report enumerates.
