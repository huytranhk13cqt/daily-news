### 🔭 Overview (Tổng)
Databricks' 24 November Data Strategy post by Ashrit Kulkarni is the first of a two-part series on data modeling for an Agentic Customer Data Platform, positioned alongside the CustomerLake product that bundles Profile and Campaign Agents with Reverse ETL connectors. The argument is that an agentic CDP needs the same raw material a traditional CDP does — curated sources, clean data, one persistent identity per customer, trusted profiles — but at the speed an agent needs to decide for one person in seconds rather than for a cohort in a daily batch. The author's thesis is that the medallion architecture still works, with one deliberate change: identity resolution runs once, after silver, and the resulting `canonical_id` becomes the join key for everything downstream. For data engineering teams building marketing, CRM or support agents on top of a lakehouse, this is a reference pattern worth reading before the second part lands with B2C and B2B use cases.

### 🔬 Details (Phân)
The medallion layout in the post is specific enough to translate into tables. Bronze is raw and append-only, pulling from apps, databases, event streams and partner tables read in place, and is also where agent responses and engagement data land back after activation. Silver normalises emails, phones and addresses, converts timestamps to UTC, applies a unified event schema and deduplicates records; it is the single home for consent history and suppression lists (unsubscribes, bounces). The identity layer sits between silver and gold and emits three artefacts — a persistent `canonical_id`, a crosswalk from every source identifier, and an `id_graph` that records the rule and confidence behind each link. Gold is then split into an entity table (one row per `canonical_id` with every activation ID), 1:1 attribute tables (model outputs, per-channel consent, derived attributes) each refreshable on its own cadence, and 1:many behaviour tables (orders, web/app events, engagement, service cases) kept at event grain with timestamps.

The identity-resolution recommendations are prescriptive: deterministic rules first (exact email, phone, partner ID), then probabilistic scoring, then clustering; tune for precision over recall because a false merge sends one person's offer to another; keep IDs persistent rather than derived from a mutable source key; and record merges and splits so the history is auditable. Survivorship rules — which source wins for each attribute — are documented per attribute, and a human reviewer samples each publish before activation. Freshness is handled by tagging every gold table as batch or event; event tables are materialised as streaming tables and can run in continuous mode for trigger-based agents, and time windows are computed against a declared as-of date so reruns are reproducible. On the platform side the post names Lakeflow Connect, Zerobus Ingest, OpenSharing, Spark Declarative Pipelines, Lakeflow Jobs, MLflow, Unity Catalog and Lakebase as the pieces holding this together.

The practical caveats in this post are unstated but worth naming for planning. There is no DDL, no SQL and no concrete schema in the article — the author describes shapes in prose and a flag table, so anyone adopting the pattern writes their own catalog, keys and constraints. `canonical_id` is a persistent surrogate: it needs a deliberate issuance process (snowflake IDs, UUID v7, or a serial sequence protected against reuse) because downstream agents treat it as the identity of record. And the batch/event flag the post recommends on each gold table is a governance signal rather than an engine feature; a continuous MLflow refresh on an "event" attribute table requires the operator to wire that cadence separately.

### 🎯 Takeaway (Hợp)
If your platform team is about to retrofit identity onto a lakehouse to feed agentic flows, lift the blueprint wholesale: identity resolution between silver and gold, `canonical_id` as the universal join key, entity/attribute/behaviour split on gold, batch/event flag per table. Keep the precision-first bias in deterministic rules and set up human-in-the-loop sampling before you connect the first Campaign Agent, because precision failures in an agentic flow are visible to the end customer within one send. Nếu đang bắt tay vào Agentic CDP đợt 2026 Q4, đây là reference model tiết kiệm nhiều cuộc họp kiến trúc trước khi Part 2 ra.

```

### 🔭 Overview (Tổng)
DuckDB's 24 November engineering blog post by Archit Dukhande, Geertjan Wielenga and Tim Cox reports that SproutMe, an early-stage AI marketing startup, cut its per-merge integration test step from a median of 639.0 seconds to 45.6 seconds — a 92.9 percent reduction — by moving its local development and CI warehouse from BigQuery to DuckDB while keeping production on BigQuery. The numbers are drawn from 88 CI/CD runs recorded over the migration period and are published as median rather than mean, which deflates the influence of occasional outliers. The architecture is the interesting part: SproutMe keeps Dagster as the orchestrator and dbt as the transformation engine, and swaps only the warehouse target at the environment boundary through a `WAREHOUSE` and `DBT_DUCKDB_PATH` pair. For data engineering teams running a dev/CI loop on a cloud warehouse and feeling the cost, latency or quota friction, this is the clearest recent case study of how a local DuckDB substitute performs over a real medallion project.

### 🔬 Details (Phân)
The migration is deliberately conservative. Production stays on BigQuery and continues to ingest roughly 365 days of history from Google Ads, Meta Ads and GA4 — millions of rows per customer at onboarding — because the team was already fluent in Google's infrastructure. Dagster orchestrates extraction from the ad and analytics sources and in dev writes directly into a DuckDB file. dbt runs against a `duckdb` target over the same medallion project: bronze is the raw sink from Dagster, silver cleans and standardises, and gold holds the user-facing models. A helper sets `TZ=UTC` because BigQuery compares timestamps in UTC while DuckDB defaults to the machine's timezone, which would otherwise make golden-file comparisons flaky across developer laptops. The CI step runs `dbt build` over the whole project against seeded fixtures, and integration tests diff selected model outputs against committed golden files so a regression fails the merge before it reaches main.

The benchmark table is the headline and worth reproducing in full. Integration test step per merge: BigQuery 639.0 s, DuckDB 45.6 s, 92.9 percent reduction. Full dbt backfill: 180.2 s vs 104.6 s, 42.0 percent reduction. Daily incremental build: 300.3 s vs 120.7 s, 59.8 percent reduction. Backfill plus daily build combined: 480.5 s vs 225.3 s, 53.1 percent reduction. These numbers are median times across five runs for the dbt figures and 88 runs for the CI step; the authors are explicit that they apply to SproutMe's development and testing workflow and are not a general engine-vs-engine benchmark. Two drivers explain most of the gap — BigQuery's slot-allocation and query-startup latency dominate short interactive runs, and DuckDB executes against a local file with no network round trip — so the ratio will narrow dramatically on workloads with larger scans and wider joins that benefit from BigQuery's distributed execution.

The migration cost is low enough to replicate. dbt's `duckdb` adapter already supports the medallion pattern end-to-end, and the only genuine portability work the post calls out is timezone discipline and keeping fixtures committed in the repo so CI reproduces what each developer sees locally. The posture the SproutMe lead signals — production on BigQuery, DuckDB confined to dev and CI unless further testing supports more — is the pragmatic read: integration tests have no SLA and modest volume, whereas production has both, and DuckDB's single-node ceiling is a real constraint on the operational side. For teams comfortable with that split, the saving on cloud-warehouse credits spent on CI is the easiest-to-defend part of this story.

### 🎯 Takeaway (Hợp)
If your team runs dbt tests against a cloud warehouse on every pull request and the numbers are visible on your monthly invoice, prototype a `duckdb` target over your medallion project this sprint behind a `WAREHOUSE` env flag and diff the outputs against a committed BigQuery or Snowflake golden; the setup is a day of work and the floor of what you save is a few hundred seconds per merge. Keep production on your incumbent warehouse until a full workload-shaped benchmark on real data confirms the single-node ceiling isn't your bottleneck. Nếu CI chạy chậm vì slot starvation, đây là route gần như không có downside — chỉ cần cẩn thận với timezone.

  target: "{{ env_var('WAREHOUSE', 'bigquery_prod') }}"
  outputs:
    bigquery_prod:
      type: bigquery
      method: service-account
      project: sproutme-prod
      dataset: analytics
      threads: 8
      location: US

    duckdb_dev:                             # local developer loop
      type: duckdb
      path: "{{ env_var('DBT_DUCKDB_PATH', './warehouse.duckdb') }}"
      threads: 4
      extensions:
        - httpfs                            # for remote fixture pulls
        - parquet

    duckdb_ci:                              # GitHub Actions runner
      type: duckdb
      path: ./ci-warehouse.duckdb           # seeded from committed fixtures
      threads: 4
```

### 🔭 Overview (Tổng)
Espressif's 25 November developer-portal post by Amey Inamdar documents a new integration between the open-source esp-webrtc-solution SDK and Agora's Software-Defined Real-Time Network (SDRTN), enabling ESP32-class devices to hold full-duplex spoken conversations with LLM-backed agents rather than the one-shot "wake, capture, upload" flows most firmware voice stacks currently use. On the device side, esp-webrtc-solution handles audio capture and playback and opens a WebRTC connection to Agora's edge; on the cloud side, developers configure agents through the Agora Agent SDK or REST APIs and the Agora Conversational AI Engine orchestrates turn-taking against the chosen model stack. For firmware teams building voice-first kiosks, smart speakers, EV-charger assistants or any BLE-adjacent device that needs a real conversational UI, this is the first Espressif-maintained open-source path that treats the voice agent as a production WebRTC peer instead of a bespoke protocol.

### 🔬 Details (Phân)
The Conversational AI Engine supports two architectures, which is the design choice teams need to decide first. The cascaded path chains a developer-selected ASR provider, an LLM and a TTS engine; the native voice-to-voice path uses models that take spoken input and emit spoken output directly, which reduces turn latency and preserves prosody at the cost of locking the stack to the providers that offer such a model. Tool use flows through the Model Context Protocol (MCP), which means an agent on this stack reaches external systems — calendars, device APIs, home-automation bridges — through the same contract used by desktop assistants, and the device becomes a thin audio peer rather than an endpoint that has to implement its own tool protocol. Agent management stays separate from the device's media connection, so adding, updating or decommissioning an agent is a cloud operation that does not reflash firmware. A no-code builder, Agora Agent Studio, is available for cascaded agents only.

The hardware and network envelope is specific. Supported chips are ESP32-S3, ESP32-S31 and ESP32-P4, with named reference boards including ESP-Mosaico (built on the ESP32-S31 and the first target platform in the post), ESP32-S31-Korvo-1 and ESP32-S3-Korvo-2 — the Korvo line is Espressif's long-running audio-dev kit family with microphone arrays, so the integration points at serious audio hardware rather than bench-top demos. Agora SDRTN advertises more than 600 points of presence, intelligent routing between them and packet-loss mitigation, which matters for IoT devices on marginal LTE or shared hotel Wi-Fi. The commercial envelope is a 300-minute Conversational AI free tier per developer; the post does not list per-minute pricing beyond that, so a production cost model has to be sourced from Agora directly before a pilot goes customer-facing.

The caveats are mostly plumbing. The post contains no code, no configuration excerpt and no concrete provider list — ASR, LLM and TTS choices inside the cascaded path are "developer's choice," and there is no benchmark on first-response latency, round-trip or dropped-fragment rate against a baseline such as a direct OpenAI Realtime WebSocket. ESP-Claw, Espressif's coding agent, is mentioned as a potential companion for voice-driven app development; that pairing is forward-looking and not part of this release. And while the integration brings a real WebRTC transport to the ESP32-S3 class, teams still need to budget microphone-array DSP, acoustic echo cancellation, keyword spotting and local audio preprocessing — the stack handles transport and orchestration, not front-end audio quality. Treat this as the start of a shipping path, not a drop-in voice-first product.

### 🎯 Takeaway (Hợp)
If you have a voice-first roadmap on ESP32-S3 or ESP32-P4 and are either hand-rolling a WebSocket uplink to a hosted LLM or waiting on a bespoke Realtime API client, run the esp-webrtc-solution + Agora path against one Korvo board this sprint and measure first-response latency against your current stack — the 300 free minutes are enough to shake out audio-quality edge cases. Decide cascaded vs native voice-to-voice early: cascaded gives provider flexibility and best-in-class cost today, but native wins on turn latency and feel for anything that reads as a conversation. Nếu team đang xoay quanh Korvo-2 hoặc ESP-Mosaico, đây là cách nhanh nhất để lên production voice agent mà không phải tự viết signalling.

### 🔭 Overview (Tổng)
CNX Software's 25 November piece by Jean-Luc Aufranc covers the launch of Pinscope, a hosted fleet-management and EU Cyber Resilience Act compliance service that spans ESP-IDF, Arduino and PlatformIO on the ESP32 family, Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W under the Pico SDK with FreeRTOS, any networked Zephyr board and Raspberry Pi/Linux gateways. The pitch is that a single outbound connection from each device gives Pinscope's cloud an HTTPS address for the device's own web server, live logs and metrics, crash backtraces across reboots, staged OTA with rollback, and a daily SBOM scan against the National Vulnerability Database and the CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue. For IoT product teams scrambling to meet the CRA's 2027 enforcement and currently stitching together a Grafana stack, a self-hosted OTA server and a spreadsheet of CVEs, this is the first consolidated commercial offering that treats CRA artefacts as first-class.

### 🔬 Details (Phân)
The transport is the sharp part of the design. Pinscope's firmware component holds a single outbound connection from each device and tunnels an HTTPS endpoint back through it, using Noise NK with X25519 and ChaCha20-Poly1305 instead of TLS; according to the article, the design works behind CGNAT and 4G without port forwarding or a VPN. The memory footprint is in the 7–10 KB RAM band — about 9.4 KB on ESP-IDF, 7 KB on ESP8266 RTOS SDK, 8.6 KB on Zephyr with MCUboot updates, and about 9 KB on Pico 2 W with Pico SDK plus FreeRTOS, HardFault decoding and A/B updates. The supported-chip list on Espressif is explicit: ESP32, S2, S3, C2, C3, C5, C6 and C61. Fleet features cover health reporting (heap, Wi-Fi signal, temperature, restart reason, task stack usage), custom metrics, searchable log capture from ESP_LOG, Zephyr LOG_*, and printf, crash reports that persist reason, task and backtrace across reboots with ELF upload for symbolicated function names and line numbers, and staged firmware rollouts to single devices or tagged groups with halt-on-first-failure semantics. Team roles (viewer, member, admin) and email or webhook alerts round out operations.

The CRA angle is where Pinscope's product thesis differs from a plain Golioth or Nabto clone. Pinscope ingests the SBOM produced by `idf.py`, `west spdx`, Yocto or `syft` for each build, checks the SBOM daily against the NVD and CISA's KEV list, and holds a VEX-style decision per CVE — which the team can enrich with an AI suggestion that an engineer then confirms. The vendor's own wording is that the product "attempts to comply" with the CRA, which is the correct cautious framing: the standard is new, implementing acts are still shifting, and VEX workflows are not yet codified. The Zephyr path is still marked beta, as are the Pico 2 W and Linux integrations, while ESP-IDF, Arduino, PlatformIO and the ESP8266 RTOS SDK are listed as stable. A free tier covers up to three devices, a 9 EUR/month tier covers up to 10, and plans continue up to 229 EUR/month.

The caveats worth internalising are real for teams considering Pinscope as a production dependency. The server component is closed source, so the data-escape risk is the vendor's uptime and jurisdiction rather than something the fleet operator controls directly — though firmware source for the ESP32, ESP8266, Pico 2 W and Zephyr targets is on GitHub for review and vendoring. The Noise NK transport is a sound protocol choice and the memory footprint is small enough to live beside a normal application stack, but a device that depends on Pinscope for OTA is a device whose update plane is vendor-managed. Integrity controls — Secure Boot, signed images, Flash Encryption on ESP32, chain-of-trust for MCUboot on Zephyr — are not provided by Pinscope and remain the firmware team's work. Treat Pinscope as a management plane on top of a well-signed firmware stack, not as a replacement for it.

### 🎯 Takeaway (Hợp)
If your 2026-Q4 roadmap has "CRA readiness" on it and you ship ESP-IDF, Arduino or Zephyr firmware, bring up the Pinscope free tier against three lab devices this week and feed it a real SBOM from your current build so the daily NVD + KEV scan can be evaluated against devices you actually ship. Keep Secure Boot, signed images and Flash Encryption on your side of the line, and treat the 9 EUR/month paid tier as the moment to decide whether a closed-source cloud dependency is acceptable for your update path. Nếu đang chưa có SBOM pipeline, đây cũng là thời điểm cài `syft` hoặc `west spdx` vào CI — chi phí thấp, lợi nhiều.
